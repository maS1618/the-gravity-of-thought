The reason you can't go faster than light is that time 'moves' only as fast as light does.

  


[NOTE: When I say 'imagine', I expect an unhindered imagination bound only by mathematics]. 

  


Imagine yourself standing on a spacestation near earth, watching a star 400 light years away with an instrument of unrestricted reach and clarity. You see a stranger (let's say Jughead) floating dangerously close to the star and buying oranges from a jolly market there.

Jughead decides to juggle these oranges and accidentally drops one into the star, where it is instantly swallowed up. 

  


Now Jughead saw the orange fall as soon as it left his hand, but- you were watching this whole incident from 400 light years away. 

This means that the light that reflected off any object or person at the market took 400 years to reach you- you saw the orange fall 400 years after Jughead did- so who's to say when the incident actually took place- with two eye witnesses telling different (but equally true) times?

  


The thing to remember through these musings is that you must have an objective reason to believe either scenario. 

  


You can see how accessible and malleable this makes time. We could ['could' is in the same category as 'imagine' - needs technological advancement] take an extremely cool camera, say, a 100 light years out, point it at earth, and watch the year 1919* repeat like a movie.

  


The important thing to realize though, is that you didn't need to take your camera, or yourself, on that journey in order for the 1919 light to reach where it did. The events hadn't irretrievably 'passed' like we believe time does. They are always there- for us to observe and (possibly) tamper with as we like. Time is not like a river. Maybe a lake. 

  


The interconnectedness of space and time are, by now, obvious. Events separated by a distance of, say, 20 light years in space, are also separated in time by 20 years. Get up. Walk from wherever you are right now to the nearest wall without moving in time - try it. 

So...you can't. You can't move in either space or time without moving in the other. 

[Before you raise objections, just existing in space counts as moving in it, i.e., if a tree is sitting there thinking- "hey! I'm only moving time but not in space!" a cyclist going by could claim that she is at rest while the tree is speeding past- along with the rest of the globe.]

  


When we want to measure the movement of a point with respect to more than one parameter, we use coordinates; which is what we want- to track the 'movement' of an event through spacetime. For simpler visualization, we can resolve space down to two dimensions and take time to be the third dimension.

At the origin of this cone, imagine there has been an explosion of light, naturally this forms a sphere of light expanding equally in all directions, but since we have taken space down to 2D, it will look like a circle getting bigger as it moves up the third axis- time. 

  


This clearly forms a conical structure whose edges expand at the speed of light. Any event [defined as a point in spacetime] taking place outside this 'light cone' cannot send or receive a signal across its borders, as nothing travels faster than light. 

All observable events are thus in this conical region, which -it must be noted- is constantly expanding. As the initial explosion gets old, more and more light reaches us from further out in spacetime.

  


So, we all live in little light cones, beyond which all events are ever present, but can only be reached by light. 

  


Oh and I suppose it's obvious that time by itself is relative -- a trick of light.
