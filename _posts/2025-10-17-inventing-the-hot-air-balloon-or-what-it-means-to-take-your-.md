Here, I define an artist as anyone who takes their artistic output seriously –

                                                                   – and argue that in society it is essential to carry with you your artistic sensibilities. To prioritise art in your concrete, “real” life just as you would other, more socially normalised elements.

  


Any reasonable adult’s (1) reaction to this claim is : “but having and keeping a stable job indicates consistency, reliability, trustworthiness. Appreciating art indicates ... your susceptibility to being distracted by brightly colored objects”.

  


My rebuttal is : “Appreciating art indicates an integrity of spirit. Your newspapers indicate symptoms of your chronic detachment from your own (brightly colored) instincts. Good luck being emotionally fulfilled in a world that frowns upon the aesthetic appreciation of trees, of butterflies that land on your suitcases, of the buildings you construct with your own hands… good luck with your hard earned detachment. I hope you don’t lose your mind. I hope I don’t read about it tomorrow morning.”

  


So, in the face of this blatant ignorance (that often ends in effectively gaslighting artists into doubting the usefulness of their work, in devaluing their own existence); in a world that does not acknowledge the reality of your ideas, your ideals - how does one remain an artist? What does the process of making something new, something beautiful, look like in such a world? How do you hold up the hypothetical value of an entity that does not yet exist – potentially never will be valued in its full essence by an average member of society?

  


_“If you wish to make an apple pie from scratch, you must first invent the universe.” – Carl Sagan, Cosmos_

Imagine, for instance, that the hot air balloon has not yet been invented. (2)

  


What does its creation require of you?

  


That the most strong, buoyant and economical balloon shape is a sphere - is not a commandment carved onto any rocks. You must discover this ideal structure yourself - most likely through trial and error - and then construct it. You must also concoct a material that is strong enough to hold you up, yet flexible enough to be bent by air, and not brittle. A thick skin. (Perhaps a realisation of how to shape the first balloon could stem from watching animal skin being filled with water from a stream or waterfall - the animal skin cloth presumably an invention early enough that we can consider it to be pre-existent). 

  


Now, in order for the balloon to float, you need to fill it with a gas less dense than the atmosphere. For this, barring even molecular theory, you must be aware that heating up a gas makes it lighter than air, that there are even different “densities” of the invisible stuff that makes up the wind against your face - not a trivial discovery; in fact, the actual inventors of the first hot air balloon, [the Montgolfier brothers](https://www.chm.bris.ac.uk/~paulmay/webprojects2003/hetherington/final/montgolfier_bros.html), were not even aware that their hot air balloon is rising due to the density gradient between hot and cold air – they experimented with many sources of gas and finally hypothesized that the “dense” smoke rising from the burning of wool, straw and old shoes was somehow pushing the balloon up. They successfully made it past their prototyping and demonstration stage (involving some airborne fowl) before the real physics was uncovered(3)  If you read about the beginnings of their journey, you will discover that it started from a place of pure curiosity and playfulness (labelled naïvette when encountered firsthand in any given generation) – Joseph Montgolfier used to watch fire and imagined that there was some kind of “force” that drove the sparks and flames upward. 

  


My point through the two avenues of balloon invention tackled so far: staying attuned to your environment and engaging with it in any way your instincts guide you is how discoveries come about. 

  


You cannot skip any step of the process of being an artist. Tirelessly, you must combat all the people that insist that this is a trivial pursuit – or worse, an escapist dream (while you are painfully aware that it is the opposite – a painstakingly detailed inventory of a reality that affords no escape). There will be no one outside of the hot air balloons inventors’ guild who will truly believe a word you say about our potential to be airborne (none of your engineering arguments will work) until you are already flying; 

          until the dream is over; 

          until it is just a set of instructions printed on what they now call a “hot-air-balloon-making manual”.

  


You cannot skip any step of the process of being an artist. Deal with the humans that stand in your way, armed with arrogance, or ignorance. How much power these people have, versus how much you give them, is perhaps a subject for another day (mais en bref: don’t give them a morsel more than they take from you by force; once you acknowledge that artistic integrity is something that exists in the real world, it becomes something to be concretely defended in said world). 

  


You cannot skip any step of the process of being an artist. Concede that not all warnings about the dangers of floating untethered in the sky are unfounded; this activity, even when everything seems to be going smoothly, can still be quite dangerous. This must be a risk you choose to take on consciously – art is not for those who willfully blind themselves to the truth. Navigating your balloon to a specific landing site requires honing a technique known as “[vertical navigation](https://seattleballooning.com/how-do-you-steer-a-hot-air-balloon-2/)”, which requires awareness of wind currents across different layers of the atmosphere, based on local terrain and weather patterns. On average, the maximum accuracy achieved by expert navigators in landings on predetermined sites is still only around 74%. 

  


So post-creation of this hot air balloon, as you are travelling over breathtaking scenery, a new question will start to nag at you – about where you’re intending to travel to – where does this journey through the atmosphere lead? 

  


In those moments, you must remind yourself – nowhere. The Earth is round, all the planets, and universes, are. That’s the only logically consistent way to define a world. (Any world, by definition, is self contained).

  


This well earned journey across the landscape is all there is (4):

  


the view – the wind – the adrenaline –

and the familiar landing sites. (5)
