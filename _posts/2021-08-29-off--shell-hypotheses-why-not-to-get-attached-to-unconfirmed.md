_Like unconfirmed hypotheses,  intermediate particles in Feynman diagrams are considered 'virtual'._  
  
Thought doesn't mean anything without relation to events. A concept without branches in, or an anchor to, reality is in reality, absent. It has no distinguishable feature from the mental ravings of a disconnected mind wandering too much - the equivalent of the disordered motion of gases in a turbulent system, the increased entropy of any physical system as it inevitably encounters a state of randomness (maybe permanently, maybe part of a bigger system that does serve a purpose, or maybe just an intermediate configuration currently meaningless). 

The physicist decides upon a question and carries out experiments to answer it. The question (which itself may be considered a concept) aims to determine physical properties of the world and is hence a system anchored in reality. Any given hypothesis in such a system, however, is not necessarily viable. Once it is found to be discordant with physical data, it must be disposed of and be stripped of any semblance of value - scientific or otherwise. 

As pointed out in an article by Sabine Hossenfelder linked [here](http://backreaction.blogspot.com/2021/07/can-physics-be-too-speculative.html), several ongoing research programs in physics are negatively affected by unscientific methodology and speculation. One feature these programs seem to share is a certain disregard for the existence - or correct treatment - of data. Without it, however, the scientific method breaks down, and we go in circles. 

Physicists (or researchers in general) hold a romantic view of the world. It is necessary - how else would someone watch electrical signals mapped out for years in end with such undiluting interest? But when one has sentimental value for a pattern recorded in nature, it is inevitable that the same emotion is carried for the hypotheses that one believes to be true, in the course of waiting for further analysis. 

So, the discipline of a researcher is also to know moving on as an artform. A worldview incorporating change is not easy to maintain. To recognize that the scientific method is a metaphorical cascade of ideas, and to get too attached to any one is a fall to serious injury. The thought that at any given moment _everything_(including most convincing ideas)_ _ is fragile. It takes away from the researcher a comfort most humans indulge- the illusion of the constant. 

The researcher's comfort, however, comes from a source not everyone can claim, stated herein - 

_If the universe were to be spontaneously reconstructed from the core and we survived the transformation, their happiness would promptly recover from the impact as they came to appreciate the brand new mysteries presented to them.  _
