"To me they look like the colors inside your head when you close your eyes" -_Two Lovers and a Bear_  
  
  


  


  


Unless time is circular, or its entropy self contained somehow, there should’ve been nothing before everything. 

  


In other words, fly me to the moon..wait what if time had a beginning, then we can infinitely extrapolate the existence of all events backwards, and thus reach a point when time hadn’t begun, which would logically imply (we can prove this in a later post; for now, trust me) that there was no light, no darkness, or anything. 

**  
**

So: How did everything begin?  

Or: What existed before anything began?

**  
**

The strained extent of my intellectual capability, unfortunately, does not yet incorporate rigorous theoretical physics. Therefore, I make no claim -and hold no delusion- of having the answer to the above questions.  

**  
**

What follows is an attempt to argue for the logical credibility of the possibility that ‘something’ i.e. the universe could have emerged out of ‘nothing’ except consciousness. This does not reflect my own belief, only a ‘widening of the philosophical circle’.

**  
**

One such demonstration could be to make matter materialize in vacuum with my mind but uh..

a less presumptuous starting point would be to create something not physical - new knowledge. 

**  
**

New Knowledge: Meaning, here, new information that was not part of the system that we began working with. For instance, if my system consists of an album by Hozier, then any knowledge imparted directly by the song lyrics is part of the system, but beyond that all knowledge is new knowledge.  

**  
**

The system I use consists of several songs, poems, quotes - all listed in the citations. Using phrases from only these (and not my own words or ideas), poetry with collective knowledge not provided in the system is emergent. Or so I think. I cannot know for sure because I am the consciousness that is part of the system - I cannot observe from within.  

**  
**

So technically, I do not know the results of this thought until observers external to the system i.e. not me, decide if new knowledge was created without using new phrases.  

**  
**

Also, since our primary aim (put simply) is to create new meaning out of old meaning, one grave error would be to use the phrases in the system out of context, such that their meaning is altered. All the phrases I use have exactly the same meaning in the poetry as they do in their original source; for instance, when I use the line ‘And indeed there will be time’ from The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock, the obvious question - “time for what?” can be answered exactly by reading the original poem by T.S. Eliot, and the same answer will be true for our ‘experimental’ poetry. There are no logical or contextual inconsistencies between the original works and this poetry. 

**  
**

To avoid using even extra punctuation, I’ve distributed different parts of the post using color. Any words written by me in this post appear in white, so do the citations .  

**  
  
**

1 To M. and D.

**  
**

2 Do you know the old language?

I do not know the old language.

Do you know the language of the old belief?

**  
**

3 Think if

understanding is

what you

had thought 

[...]of it,

**  
**

4 Can blaze be done in cochineal,

Or noon in mazarin?

**  
**

5 How deeply are you sleeping

Or are you still awake?

Be careful [...]

Be careful what it takes;

What I’ve seen so far -

The good ones always seem to break.

**  
**

6 Yes, and how many years can a mountain exist 

Before it’s washed to the sea?

**  
**

7 I don't think you understand!

There's nowhere left to turn

Walls keep breaking

**  
**

8 So

**  
**

9 Weep little lion man 

Rate yourself and rake yourself 

Take all the courage you have left. 

Your boldness stands alone among the wreck:

**  
**

10 “I’ve walked and I’ve crawled on six crooked highways

I’ve stepped in the middle of seven sad forests

I’ve been out in front of a dozen dead oceans.”

**  
**

11 trying to write a song so articulate

It could right my wrongs but I wrote it wrong

(And what did it mean?)

That I got it wrong.

**  
  
**

12 I missed it deliberately.

**  
**

13 We paint a sky

The brightest blue

**  
**

14 Reflecting the rainbow of a thunderstorm

Which in a distant valley has been staged -

For we are most artistically caged

**  
**

15 “Run, run, lost boy”

When we’re bored we play in the woods

**  
**

16 And indeed there will be time.

**  
**

17(Someday someone must be going my way home)

**  
**

18 Homesick for steadfast honey,

Ah! the bee flies not

That brews that rare variety.

**  
**

19 So if you have a minute why don’t we go

**  
**

20 To a place [...] we could be anyone

Bottle up our fears - Brew a taste so sweet

Knock us off our feet

**  
**

   21 the rite of movement-

Its reasonin' made lucid and cool

**  
**

22 The felt presence of immediate experience.

(this is what has been stolen from you)

We’re always about to be happy

Or about to be free

**  
**

23 Is the grass of Eden overrated?

**  
  
**

24 At Eve’s grave

ADAM: Wheresoever she was, there was Eden.

**  
**

I know that the meaning of this poetry would take a lot of work to understand. A perfect understanding, in fact, would need one to follow up on every one of the citations (thus understanding the system), and then put together contextually unharmed meanings together in the order and seamless continuity they are portrayed in.  

**  
**

Before you provide your opinion on the validity of this experiment, remember how important your role is. You are the external observer that I cannot be.
