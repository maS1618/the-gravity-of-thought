Flavors (types) of quarks (a kind of elementary particle)  
  
  


  


  


A proton has 3 valence quarks - 2 _up_ quarks and 1 _down_ quark.  

That is to say, it can have a lot of quarks and anti-quarks, but after they cancel each other out you should be left with 2 up quarks, 1 down quark and all the other flavors should annihilate each other completely. 

  


Quarks have charges, but not electrical- color charges. 

Quarks can be of 3 colors- blue, green, or red. Their anti-quarks are respectively anti-blue, anti-green, and anti-red. You'll see why.

These are obviously not real colors- since quarks are too small to reflect visible light- they are a type of charge {like positive or negative electric charge}, that is most stable in the colorless (white) state.

Likewise, the strong nulear force that holds quarks together is caused by the exchange of gluons {the force carriers for Strong Nuclear Force}. 

  


Gluons and photons behave differently though. 

Gluons, like quarks, also have a color charge- but it's a little bit of a mess. Quarks have pure color charges, but each gluon has a color and a different anti-color charge, for example: [blue + anti-red], or [green+ anti-blue]. 

  


Therefore while photons (being electrically neautral packets of energy) pass smoothly through each other, gluons attract other gluons.

The field lines grow sparce as they move farther apart  
  


So electromagnetic force grows weaker with increasing distance between the charges {since it relies completely on the 2 charges which are being pulled apart}, but the Strong Nuclear Force remains just as strong over greater distances because because the gluons themselves keep attracting each other while being exchanged. 

  
This exchange of gluons keeps the color of each quark changing but its flavor (type) remains the same. This is what holds the quarks together- keeping the color charge conserved, such that the combination is color neutral.

The tunnel-like thing connecting the quarks is called a flux tube [cuz it's made of gluon field lines]  
  
There's also a reason we don't have 'white' quarks. We never have- or will- observe an isolated quark. As we try to pull the 2 quarks apart, the huge amount of energy we need to supply for the process ends up making a virtual particle which pairs off with opposite ends of the momentarily freed quark.  
{Kinda like how we can never only observe the north pole of a magnet without the south pole}

Since there's a lot of energy within protons and neutrons [due to quarks rushing about and the gluon field fluctuations], virtual particles (combinations of quarks and anti-quarks) are constantly appearing and disappearing*. These virtual particles can be transferred between the neutrons and protons, effectively binding them together in the nucleus. However, because virtual particles can exist for very little time, this is a short range force.

  


*because matter and anti-matter together make energy, therefore energy makes matter + anti-matter.
