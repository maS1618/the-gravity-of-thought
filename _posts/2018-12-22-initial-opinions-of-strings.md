_  
_ Everything is made up of rubber bands, figuratively. Literally it's made up of strings.  
  
(Sorry, hitchhikers- turns out [Answer to the Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrases_from_The_Hitchhiker%27s_Guide_to_the_Galaxy#Answer_to_the_Ultimate_Question_of_Life,_the_Universe,_and_Everything_\(42\)) isn't 42. It's strings. Unless, of course, the question was how many strings make up the universe.)   
  
So here's what happened. We were studying mesons  (particles made of quarks and antiquarks) and realized that their interactive behavior resembled that of two particles tied together by a string. Physicists hypothesized that the strings were some sort of vibrating elastic bands made up of gluons (elementary particles that cause the strong inter-quark force). However, this description led to unexpected fluctuations in the gluon force-field. So, we gave up the idea- at least, used something else to describe mesons.   
  
The fluctuations in the gluon field, though, suggested the presence of a particle suspiciously similar to one we recognized- gravitons (the force carriers for gravitation). Gravitation had emerged elegantly, almost naturally, from the mathematics of strings. Obviously, all other forces should too. Hopefully, they would.   
  
Thus began speculations on the parameters of the string.

When we take a string in a loop (or with its ends fixed at two points) they handle waves rather magnificently.   
  
Suppose you vibrate one end of the string, thus creating a wave. This wave will successively move around and collide with itself till it either enhances or diminishes, undergoing destructive or constructive [interference](https://study.com/academy/lesson/constructive-and-destructive-interference.html) (_as illustrated_).    
  
  
  
What this means is, only for very specific frequencies of vibration, will the wave stay alive. Most times it will just cancel itself out. These specific frequencies each correspond to a certain fixed energy (Quantised energy, intervalled frequencies...Sound like quantum mechanics yet?).    
When physicists noticed these similarities between the strings and quantum mechanics, they realized they could scale down the size of the string to [Plank's length](https://www.physlink.com/Education/AskExperts/ae281.cfm), throw in a few more dimensions and voila! The String Theory can now describe not just gravitons, but all force-carrying particles, which would be created at different frequencies of vibration of the same fundamental strings. When one string can't do the job, they can even split or merge, creating all the particles that make up _anything_.  
  
So.. we've ended up with 9 spatial dimensions +1 for time +1 for M theory _(some other day)    _  
  
Where are the other 6 spatial dimensions? Well, it's possible they're negligibly small, so that they just put us back where we started very quickly so we don't notice them (like an ant walking in a very thin box).  
  
 Or maybe we're just three-dimensional shadows in a nine-dimensional universe.   
  
Maybe we're just exceptionally fun shadows.
