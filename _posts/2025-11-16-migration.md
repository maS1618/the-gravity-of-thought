The tota (_rose-ringed parakeet_)* in a tree outside my balcony [08 Nov '25]  
  
The bird I saw 

in the cheerful leaves of autumn 

\- Psittacula krameri -

the rose ringed parakeet.

  


So lemon-green

I could only really spot it when it flew from one branch to another.

The tree – in the throes of autumn – slightly damp, also heavy lemon yellow.

  


They conferenced internally 

in their little autumnal hollow of sweet perfumed air, 

homely rustling music;

  


 Their lives softly mellowed

(and their existence carried)

by that merry burst of energy that comes 

from being at exactly the right place at the right time, 

doing exactly

  


what your soul desires. 

  


Given this delicate balance of prerequisite factors, 

it is only natural that 

as time passes

  


this divine Alignment no longer holds true. 

  


The sun sets

the leaves turn a deeper orange

and no longer camouflage 

the parakeet who could not keep up with this transformation.

  


The bird must take the bright yellow of its feathers elsewhere – to greener pastures (haha, pun intended);

must find another place that disguises it 

into safety;

a place where he seamlessly fits in once again.

  


Migration is the act of finding trees that match the hue of your feathers.

  


A place where you are not vanished, but engulfed – 

Where your brightness is recognized 

as a labor of _beauty_ ,

rather than 

a glaring inconvenience 

contesting survival. 

  


Migration brings you face to face with :

  


precisely how rare 

the gradient of your feathers

is.

  


And: with winter 

(when all your chlorophylled points of reference vanish, and you are flying blind over a bare landscape),

  


And: with sunsets 

(when the tree you have come to love, slowly, inexplicably, changes color).

  


It dawns on you 

that you must find a part of yourself that shines

– like the only constant backdrop in the dark night –

and wear it on your sleeve

// or //

 spend half your life 

not belonging to this star-filled scenery.

  


That is to say, the scene often changes you;

more than you change it.

  


But.

That tree was just a tree until the parakeet arrived –

and then it was a _nook_. 

  


A nook: with music, flying, and a warm body to justify the warmth of this retreat – 

Hellfire for colder creatures.

i.e., 

your connection with a place justifies its existence –

reinforces its creation.
